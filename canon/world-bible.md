# World bible

Status: Approved by the owner, 2026-10-09 (decisions/0008). A wider name-clash check was done on 2026-10-10 ([docs/name-check.md](../docs/name-check.md)): "the Underroot" clashes with existing games and is awaiting the owner's decision on a new name; everything else is clear or generic.

## The premise

A tree stands at the edge of the world, so large that from far away it is the colour of the sky. People call it **the Godtree**, because a god is said to live in it. Nobody who tells the story has been there.

Its roots run across the whole land, each one wider than a river. Where a root passes, the land takes on a character of its own. A road follows the greatest root from the far meadows to the trunk. This is **the Rootway**, and the game is the walk along it.

## The Woken

Now and then one of the Godtree's seed-husks, each as big as a house, drifts down somewhere along the Rootway. Inside each husk is a person, asleep. They wake knowing how to speak and how to hold a tool, and nothing else. These are **the Woken**. Every player is one.

- The folk who were born here call themselves **the Rooted**. They farm, trade and keep the towns. Most are kind to the Woken; some are wary, because the Woken always leave for the tree.
- Why the tree sends the Woken, nobody knows. Every land has its own answer.
- A defeated Woken does not die. They fall asleep and wake again at the nearest **Cradle**, a small husk-shrine. The Rooted do not get that mercy, which is why they leave the dungeons to the Woken.

## The god

Nobody in the world knows what is in the Godtree, and neither does the player. Each land along the Rootway holds a different belief about it, and each belief shapes that land's town, quests and boss.

**What is actually in the tree is reserved for the owner.** Contributors do not write, hint at or depict the answer. Proposals may add beliefs about the god; they may not settle it.

## Weaving (magic)

The Godtree sheds drifting points of light called **motes**. Most people only see them. Some Woken can gather motes with their hands and tie them into shapes of light called **knots**. Tying knots is **weaving**, and those who do it are **weavers**.

- A knot is made with a hand gesture, held or thrown. Bigger effects need two hands or several knots in a row.
- Weaving is physical. A knot has to be aimed, placed or pressed against something; it obeys pillar 1.
- Motes are thick near roots and Cradles and thin deep underground, which is why dungeons are dangerous for weavers who do not plan.
- Healing is weaving too: a healer ties a knot onto a friend.

## The road, zone by zone

Only Zone 1 is drafted in detail. Later zones are one-line sketches to be developed when their turn comes, and they will change.

### Zone 1: the Cradle Meadows (working name)

Rolling green meadow under a huge sky, scattered with old seed-husks, the Godtree small and blue on the horizon. The gentlest land on the road, where every Woken starts.

- **Town: Huskhaven.** Built in and around the largest fallen husk. Lantern-lit, patched together, welcoming. The Rooted here believe the god is a **gardener** and the Woken are its seedlings, so they treat newcomers like children who need feeding.
- **Wilderness: the Windlow.** Open meadow, low stone walls, wind-bent trees, shallow streams. Teaches fighting, blocking and travelling.
- **Enemies.** *Burrlings*: knee-high rolling seed-burrs that swarm and bounce. *Thornbacks*: bristled, boar-like chargers that teach dodging and shield work. *Barkbound*: empty suits of old armour grown through with bark, the first enemies that parry back.
- **Dungeon: the Underroot.** Caves where a root tip has broken through the rock. Pale wood, hanging root-hair, pools of motes, then darkness.
- **Boss: the Rootwarden.** A towering guardian of root-wood and stone that stands in the gap where the Rootway leaves the meadows. Slow, heavy, readable limbs made to be blocked, parried and climbed past. It is not evil; it tests whether a Woken is ready for the road.

### Later zones (sketches only)

| 2 | The Saltglass Shallows | Tidal flats and mirror-bright shallows where a root drinks the sea | The god is a tide: it gives and takes back |

**Huskhaven is also the shared hub town** where parties gather before entering a zone.

## Naming rules

- Places and creatures are named in plain compound words a child could say: Huskhaven, Windlow, Thornback. No apostrophes, no invented alphabets, no real-world mythological names (no Norse, Greek or other pantheon terms).
- People among the Rooted have short, soft, two-syllable names (Mira, Tobin, Hale). The Woken choose their own.
- Never use a name, character, place or design from an existing work. The automated checks carry a list of the obvious ones; the rule covers everything.
