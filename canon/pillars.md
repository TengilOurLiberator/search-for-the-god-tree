# Design pillars

Status: Approved by the owner, 2026-10-09 (decisions/0008). Only the owner changes these.

Every proposal, brief and review is tested against these five. An idea that breaks a pillar is declined, however good it is on its own.

## 1. Your hands are the game

Outcomes come from what the player's hands physically do. A block works because the shield was in the way; a parry works because the blades met. No canned hit reactions, no dice deciding whether a swing connected, no button that plays an attack.

- Test: could a player get this result by pressing a button instead of moving? If yes, redesign it.
- Stats and gear change weight, reach, sturdiness and damage. They never replace the swing.

## 2. The Godtree is always ahead

The tree is visible from almost everywhere outdoors and grows nearer with every zone. The player always knows where they are going and can see how far they have come.

- Test: standing in this spot, can the player find the tree, or be surprised by it when they step outside?
- Every zone changes how the tree looks: bigger, differently lit, seen from a new angle.

## 3. Better together

The game is built for a party. Roles emerge from gear and skills and need each other: someone holds the line, someone keeps people standing, someone ends the fight.

- Test: does this give players a reason to look at, help or rely on each other?
- The wilderness can be walked alone. Dungeons and bosses expect friends.

## 4. Bright world, real danger

The world is colourful, hopeful and readable. The danger is honest: dungeons and bosses can and do defeat a careless party.

- Test: can a player read this enemy, attack or hazard at a glance, from its silhouette and colour alone?
- Wonder first. No gore, no grimdark, no cynicism. Fear comes from stakes, not from ugliness.

## 5. Comfort is part of the design

A player who feels sick stops playing. Comfort options (vignette, snap turn, teleport) are first-class, and frame rate is a feature.

- Test: does this hold 90 frames per second on the target hardware, and can a comfort-sensitive player still do it?
- Never take control of the player's head. Never move the world without the player's input unless they can opt out.
